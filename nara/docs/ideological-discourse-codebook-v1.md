# NARA Ideological Discourse Codebook v1

## Purpose
This codebook operationalizes the ideological-discourse analysis of Al-Naba weekly editorials for research use. It is grounded in the doctoral dissertation methodology supplied by the researcher and extends it with additional reproducible variables for longitudinal analysis.

## Core analytical unit
Primary unit: the idea/theme unit (وحدة الفكرة). An idea may occur in a sentence, paragraph, or a larger passage. One editorial may contain multiple coded ideas. Coding should retain the original quotation location (issue/page/paragraph) but analytical outputs should paraphrase extremist rhetoric unless verbatim quotation is methodologically necessary.

Add longitudinal strength: absent / weak / moderate / strong.

## D. Additional longitudinal variables
### D1. Core ideological proposition
One-sentence analytical paraphrase of the editorial's central ideological claim.

### D2. In-group / out-group construction
- definition of the in-group
- definition of the out-group
- boundary markers
- conditions for inclusion/exclusion
- degree of absolutism

### D3. Enemy taxonomy
Code who is constructed as the principal adversary and why: doctrinal, political, military, sectarian, national, international, intra-jihadist, or mixed.

### D4. Legitimacy mechanism
How the editorial seeks legitimacy:
- textual/religious authority
- historical precedent
- claimed military success
- governance/order
- victimhood/grievance
- moral superiority
- inevitability/divine destiny
- organizational continuity

### D5. Statehood and governance position
- caliphate/state claim
- governance performance
- territorial control
- clandestine/organizational continuity
- rejection of nation-state/borders
- law/sharia governance
- institutional authority

### D6. Violence legitimation intensity
Research-only scale:
0 none
1 indirect/abstract
2 justificatory but non-specific
3 explicit approval/glorification
4 central organizing theme
This variable measures rhetoric; it must never be transformed into operational guidance.

### D7. Mobilization intensity
0 none
1 devotional/morale only
2 identity reinforcement
3 explicit mobilization/recruitment appeal
4 sustained mobilization as dominant purpose

### D8. Emotional register
- fear
- anger
- grievance
- humiliation
- revenge
- triumph
- patience/endurance
- hope/reward
- moral certainty
- nostalgia/restoration
- mixed

### D9. Temporal orientation
- past/restoration
- present confrontation
- future victory/eschatology
- continuity across eras

### D10. Geographic imagination
- local
- national
- regional
- transnational/global
- anti-border/territorially universalist

### D11. Relationship between editorial discourse and operations in same issue
- no evident link
- thematic alignment
- enemy alignment
- geographic alignment
- retrospective legitimation
- anticipatory mobilization
No causal claim should be made without independent evidence.

### D12. Change from previous issues
- stable
- intensified
- moderated
- topic shift
- enemy shift
- frame shift
- new ideological reference
- return to earlier theme

## E. Verification and source discipline
The editorial is a primary propaganda source, not an independent factual authority. Separate:
- what the editorial claims
- what NARA infers analytically
- what external sources corroborate
No ideological or operational assertion is to be recorded as established fact solely because it appears in Al-Naba.

## F. Coding reliability
For each variable store:
- coder/engine
- confidence score (0-1)
- evidence page/paragraph
- rationale
- ambiguity flag
A sample should be double-coded periodically and intercoder agreement assessed before large-scale longitudinal comparisons.

## G. Output levels
Weekly: editorial theme, dominant frame, actors, ideological references, objectives, notable change.
Monthly: frequency distributions and qualitative synthesis.
Quarterly: trend shifts and recurring ideological clusters.
Annual: longitudinal evolution, stability/change, frame distributions, actor/enemy shifts, and relationship to declared operational geography.

## Research ethics note
Outputs should use neutral analytical language, attribute claims to the source, avoid reproducing propaganda unnecessarily, and avoid generating material that facilitates violence, recruitment, evasion, or targeting.
