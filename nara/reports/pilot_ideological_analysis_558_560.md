# NARA Pilot: Ideological Discourse Analysis — Issues 558–560

## Scope and method
This pilot applies the NARA ideological-discourse codebook to the editorials of Al-Naba issues 558, 559 and 560. The unit of analysis is the idea/theme, following the doctoral-study methodology. The coding separates direct textual description from analyst inference. Scores are analytical aids, not claims about causation or effectiveness.

## Issue 558 — «الكفارات والدرجات»
**Primary topic:** religious, devotional and behavioral guidance.

**Core idea:** the editorial constructs a moral-spiritual scheme in which religious practices, patience and worship function as means of expiation and elevation in rank. The message addresses the believer as an individual moral actor rather than constructing an immediate political or military enemy.

**Ideological references:** Qur'an and Prophetic tradition are the dominant legitimating references.

**Actors:** the believer/Muslim, the self, temptation and spiritual weakness.

**Communication goals:** preaching, moral discipline, reinforcement of devotional identity.

**Frame:** primarily ethical/moral, with individual responsibility as a secondary frame.

**Self/other structure:** relatively low-polarization. The principal contrast is between disciplined belief and weakness/gaflah rather than an external political enemy.

**Violence legitimation:** 0/4.

**Mobilization:** 1/4.

**Analytical classification:** ideological maintenance / internal religious socialization.

## Issue 559 — «صراع العقيدة»
**Primary topic:** doctrinal conflict, political interpretation and mobilizing discourse.

**Core idea:** the editorial explicitly rejects national, material and liberation-based explanations of conflict and reframes it as a permanent struggle of identity and creed. It constructs a binary worldview organized around belief/unbelief and Islam/jahiliyya, while treating political and territorial motives as secondary.

**Ideological references:** Qur'anic citations, scriptural-historical analogies and exegetical reference.

**Actors:** the organization and its claimed fighters/supporters; the United States; governments and systems categorized as hostile; broader categories of 'enemies of religion'.

**Communication goals:** ideological interpretation, mobilization, delegitimization of compromise, and legitimation of armed struggle.

**Frames:** conflict is dominant; strategy and responsibility are secondary.

**Self/other structure:** maximal polarization. The text constructs mutually exclusive moral-political camps and denies the legitimacy of national or negotiated frameworks.

**Violence legitimation:** 4/4.

**Mobilization:** 4/4.

**Geographic orientation:** explicitly transnational; the text rejects territorial boundaries as limiting the ideological struggle.

**Change from issue 558:** sharp escalation from inward devotional discourse to outward conflict framing, enemy construction and militant mobilization.

**Analytical classification:** high-intensity conflict legitimization / transnational ideological mobilization.

## Issue 560 — «جلاء القلوب»
**Primary topic:** religious, devotional and behavioral guidance.

**Core idea:** the editorial diagnoses spiritual heedlessness as a product of material preoccupation, information overload and sin, and presents remembrance, worship and disciplined religious practice as the remedy.

**Ideological references:** Qur'an, Prophetic tradition and Ibn al-Qayyim.

**Actors:** the Muslim individual, the contemporary social environment, the self and spiritual distractions.

**Communication goals:** preaching, moral reform, identity maintenance and religious discipline.

**Frames:** ethical/moral is dominant; responsibility and human concern are secondary.

**Self/other structure:** low external polarization; the primary opposition is between remembrance and heedlessness, spiritual discipline and material distraction.

**Violence legitimation:** 0/4.

**Mobilization:** 1/4.

**Change from issue 559:** substantial de-escalation in militant rhetoric and a return to internal moral-religious socialization.

**Analytical classification:** ideological maintenance / devotional discipline.

## Cross-issue findings
The three-editorial pilot shows a useful distinction between two recurring discourse modes:

1. **Internal ideological maintenance:** issues 558 and 560 focus on individual religious discipline, devotional practice, spiritual purification and reinforcement of identity.
2. **External conflict mobilization:** issue 559 transforms the editorial into a doctrinal explanation of political conflict, constructs a highly polarized enemy image, rejects national framing and elevates armed struggle into an ideological obligation.

This demonstrates why NARA should not label every editorial simply as 'extremist rhetoric' or 'mobilization'. The system should first identify the communication function and then score specific dimensions such as enemy construction, mobilization and violence legitimation.

## Pilot coding rules confirmed
- The **idea/theme** remains the principal coding unit.
- Qur'an, hadith, scholars and organizational sources should be coded both as references and by **function**: legitimation, identity construction, enemy construction, behavioral instruction, or conflict justification.
- Conflict framing should be coded separately from violence legitimation: a text may discuss conflict without explicitly legitimizing violence.
- Mobilization intensity should distinguish devotional discipline from outward political/militant mobilization.
- Temporal comparison should record escalation, de-escalation, thematic shift and return to prior ideological motifs.
- Correlation between editorial discourse and operations in the same issue must be recorded only as thematic co-occurrence unless independent evidence supports a causal connection.

## Next validation step
The next pilot phase should apply the same codebook independently a second time (human coder or delayed recoding), calculate agreement per variable, refine ambiguous categories, and only then use the codebook for automated weekly production.
